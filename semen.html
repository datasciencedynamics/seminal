WHO 2021 Semen Reference Distributions

The 6th edition of the WHO laboratory manual (2021) replaced the 2010 reference values with the distribution reported by Campbell et al. (2021): semen parameters from 3,589 men in 12 countries whose partners conceived within 12 months. seminal.semen carries that table exactly as printed and answers two questions about a result: where it sits among fertile men, and whether it is below the conventional lower reference limit (the 5th centile).

The paper is explicit that the 5th centile is not a threshold between fertile and infertile men; it is the point below which only 5% of fertile men fall.

Where a value sits

from seminal import semen_percentile

semen_percentile("concentration", 36)   # 25.0
semen_percentile("progressive_motility", 55)   # 50.0
semen_percentile("normal_forms", 2)     # 2.5  (below the lowest published centile)

The percentile is linearly interpolated between the nine published centiles (2.5, 5, 10, 25, 50, 75, 90, 95, 97.5) and clipped to that range, since the tails are not published. It is exact at the nine points and an approximation between them.

Lower reference limits

from seminal import lower_reference_limit, below_reference

lower_reference_limit("concentration")             # 16
lower_reference_limit("concentration", "who2010")  # 15
below_reference("total_motility", 40)              # True (limit 42)

edition="who2010" gives the 5th-edition limits (Cooper et al. 2010) for labs that still report against them.

Cohorts

add_semen_percentile_cols takes a DataFrame with one row per semen analysis and a mapping of parameter name to column name. For each mapped column it adds a percentile column (_pct, 2.5 to 97.5) and a below-limit flag (_below_lrl, below the WHO 5th centile).

from seminal import add_semen_percentile_cols

df["tsc"] = df["vol_ml"] * df["conc"]        # total number, if not recorded

scored = add_semen_percentile_cols(
    df,
    columns={
        "volume": "vol_ml",
        "concentration": "conc",
        "total_number": "tsc",
        "progressive_motility": "pr",
        "normal_forms": "morph",
    },
    edition="who2020",                       # or "2010_2020"
)
# adds vol_ml_pct, vol_ml_below_lrl, conc_pct, conc_below_lrl, ...
scored["n_below_lrl"] = scored.filter(like="_below_lrl").sum(axis=1)

If the columns already carry the parameter names (volume, concentration, …) the mapping can be omitted.

<col>_pct is a float from 2.5 to 97.5 (the man’s centile among fertile men); <col>_below_lrl is a nullable boolean, True below the WHO 5th centile. Any subset of the nine parameters can be mapped; nothing else is required of the DataFrame.

Whether the scored columns mean anything on a new dataset depends on three things:

  • Units. They must match PARAMETERS[name]["unit"]: mL, 106/mL, 106 per ejaculate, and percentages on a 0 to 100 scale. A motility column stored as a fraction (0.55) scores as the 2.5th centile.

  • Morphology. The reference was scored with strict (Tygerberg) criteria. Results under older WHO criteria run several times higher and are not comparable.

  • Missing values. Unparseable values become NaN in the percentile column and <NA> in the flag column; do not fill them before scoring.

Two uses are worth naming. Describing a cohort against fertile men: the median _pct per parameter and the share _below_lrl, which is about 5% in a fertile reference population and far higher in an infertility clinic. And a simple severity count, n_below_lrl (0 to 9). The percentiles are a monotone transform of the raw values, so they add nothing to tree-based models; they put the nine parameters on one scale for plots and linear models.

Direction matters for immotile: the flag fires when the immotile fraction is below the 5th centile, the favourable end.

The quickstart notebook runs this on a toy export; section 7 of Reproducing Campbell et al. 2021 (WHO 2021 reference distribution) scores the reference men themselves (5% flag overall, with wide variation by study).

Things to know

  • The parameters are those of a standard semen analysis under the WHO manual: volume, concentration, total number, total and progressive motility, non-progressive, immotile, vitality (eosin or hypo-osmotic swelling) and strict-criteria normal morphology. pH, round cells and other measurements have no published distribution.

  • The reference men are fertile by definition (time to pregnancy up to 12 months), so the distribution describes fertile men, not the general population and not an infertility clinic.

  • Methodological differences between contributing laboratories are part of the spread; Paffoni et al. (2022) argue the pooled studies cannot be treated as one population. The centiles are reported here as WHO adopted them.

  • The library does not interpret results. A value at the 3rd centile is a value at the 3rd centile; what it means for a couple depends on everything else about them.
